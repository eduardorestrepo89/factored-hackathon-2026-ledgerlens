# LATAM Regulatory & Operational Rules Constraining AI Customer-Service Workflows in Retail Banking (Mexico, Colombia, Argentina, Brazil)

> Research date: 2026-09-29. Purpose: raw material for a clearly labelled **synthetic policy service** (deterministic rules a hackathon AI agent must obey). Not legal advice. Wherever a figure was read directly from the primary legal text it is marked **[primary text verified]**; figures taken only from secondary summaries (news, law-firm blogs, search snippets) are marked **[secondary]**. Day-count types matter: "días hábiles / dias úteis" = business days; "días naturales / dias corridos" = calendar days; where the law does not say, it is flagged.

## 1. Complaint handling (PQR / reclamos / queixas): statutory deadlines, escalation paths, required content of a formal response

### Takeaway
All four countries impose hard first-level response clocks: **Colombia 15 business days** (entity) with an 8+8 business-day Defensor del Consumidor Financiero (DCF) track; **Mexico 30 business days** at the bank's UNE; **Argentina 10 business days** (BCRA), after which the customer can go to the BCRA; **Brazil 7 calendar days** at the SAC (Decreto 11.034/2022), **10 business days** at the Ouvidoria (Res. CMN 4.860/2020) and 10 days on consumidor.gov.br. Every regime requires a traceable claim/protocol number and a written, complete, reasoned answer.

### Cited Findings

**Colombia (SFC / DCF / SmartSupervision)**
- Supervised entities must answer a petition/complaint within **15 (business) days** of receipt, per art. 14 of the Administrative Procedure Code as replaced by **Ley 1755 de 2015** (derecho de petición) [secondary] — [SFC FAQ 3: Derechos de petición ante entidades vigiladas](https://www.superfinanciera.gov.co/preguntas-frecuentes/3/3-derechos-de-peticion-ante-entidades-vigiladas/); [SFC — Quejas contra entidades vigiladas](https://www.superfinanciera.gov.co/publicaciones/10087423/consumidor-financieroinformacion-generalquejas-contra-entidades-vigiladas-10087423/)
- Habeas-data PQRs (credit-bureau reporting disputes) must be resolved within **15 business days**, extendable by up to **8 more business days**; the SFC reinforced this 15-business-day limit for supervised entities in Feb 2024 [secondary] — [Ámbito Jurídico](https://www.ambitojuridico.com/noticias/general/administrativo-y-contratacion/peticiones-quejas-o-reclamos-relacionados-con-habeas); [Infobae, 27 Feb 2024](https://www.infobae.com/colombia/2024/02/27/superintendencia-financiera-dio-15-dias-para-contestar-quejas-y-reclamos-por-habeas-data-por-aumento-de-casos/)
- If the consumer complains to the **SFC** itself, the SFC's term to *start* processing is **15 business days**; there is no fixed deadline to finish (depends on complexity: entity reply, evaluation, further requests) — [SFC FAQ 4: Quejas — competencia de la SFC](https://www.superfinanciera.gov.co/preguntas-frecuentes/4/4-quejas-contra-entidades-vigiladas-competencia-de-la-sfc/)
- **DCF procedure — Decreto 2555 de 2010, art. 2.34.2.1.5** [primary text verified, as reproduced in an entity's published procedure updated 22 Oct 2025] — [DCF procedure (Cooperativa JFK)](https://www.jfk.com.co/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Procedimiento-para-la-Resolucion-de-Quejas-o-Reclamos-por-parte-del-DCF.pdf):
  - Complaint filed at a branch must be forwarded by the entity to the DCF within **3 business days**.
  - DCF decides admission/competence and notifies both parties within **3 business days** (must mention the option of a conciliation hearing at any time).
  - If DCF needs more info, the party must supply it within the term set by DCF, **max 8 business days**; then DCF decides admission within **3 business days**; failure by the consumer = complaint deemed withdrawn (desistida), can be refiled.
  - Entity must answer the DCF within **8 business days** from the transfer (extendable at DCF discretion; entity must tell the consumer the reasons for the extension). The answer must be "**completa, clara y suficiente**" and state whether the entity accepts that an unfavourable DCF decision is binding.
  - DCF decides within **max 8 business days** after the entity's term expires; decision must be "**motivada, clara y completa**" and communicated to both parties the **next business day**.
  - Entity may rectify at any time before decision; DCF then asks the consumer (within **3 business days**) and the consumer has **8 business days** to reply; silence = rectification accepted.
  - DCF must escalate matters of "general interest" to the SFC; conciliation agreements have res judicata / enforceable effect.
- The procedure is also referenced in the **Circular Básica Jurídica (CE 029/2014)**; one bank manual cites "Parte I, Título IV, Capítulo II, numeral 8.2.3" [secondary; numeral not verified] — [SFC Circular Básica Jurídica](https://www.superfinanciera.gov.co/publicaciones/10083443/normativanormativa-generalcircular-basica-juridica-ce-10083443/); [GNB Sudameris DCF procedure](https://www.gnbsudameris.com.co/content/files/docs/Procedimiento_Presentar_Queja_Defensor.pdf)
- **SmartSupervision**: SFC **Circular Externa 023 de 2021** set instructions for the SmartSupervision tool and the SAC, creating a new structure for attending and reporting complaints so the SFC has near-real-time data on complaint handling by supervised entities — [CERLATAM: CE 023/2021](https://www.cerlatam.com/normatividad/superfinanciera-circular-externa-023-de-2021/); [ACIS](https://acis.org.co/portal/content/noticiasdelsector/superfinanciera-adopta-transici%C3%B3n-smartsupervision-para-gesti%C3%B3n-de-pqr%C2%B4s)
- Recent (Aug 2026): SFC activated "Quejas exprés" to prioritise complaints after an earthquake, plus temporary credit relief [headline only; details not read] — [Infobae, 13 Aug 2026](https://www.infobae.com/colombia/2026/08/13/la-superfinanciera-activo-las-quejas-expres-para-priorizar-reclamos-tras-el-sismo-y-anuncio-alivios-temporales-de-credito-para-los-afectados/)

**Mexico (UNE / CONDUSEF / SIPRES / REDECO)**
- **LPDUSF art. 50 Bis**: every financial institution must have a **Unidad Especializada de Atención a Usuarios (UNE)** that receives complaints by any means (including branches) and must **respond in writing within ≤30 business days** from receipt — [Art. 50 Bis LPDUSF (compendium)](https://sdv.com.mx/compendio/ley-de-proteccion-y-defensa-al-usuario-de-servicios-financieros/articulo-50-bis/); [LPDUSF (CONDUSEF PDF)](https://www.condusef.gob.mx/documentos/marco_legal/LPDUSF_090318.pdf)
- The UNE data notice registered with CONDUSEF must state the maximum response periods (complaints: 30 business days) — [CONDUSEF Disposición en materia de Registros](https://www.condusef.gob.mx/documentos/marco_legal/DispRegistrosAnte-CONDUSEF.pdf)
- For disputed movements on a statement ("aclaraciones"), **LTOSF art. 23** [primary text verified, Profeco copy last reformed DOF 25-05-2010; later reforms may exist]: client may file within **90 calendar days** from the statement cut-off date (or from the operation); at the branch or the UNE, in writing/email/any provable means; **institution must acknowledge receipt**; for amounts drawn via CONDUSEF-defined mechanisms (e.g., cards) client may **withhold payment of the disputed amount** and related amounts until resolved; institution has **max 45 days** (type of day not specified in the text) to deliver a **written dictamen signed by authorised staff**, with copy of the evidence and a **detailed report answering every fact** in the request; **up to 180 calendar days** for operations made abroad; if the charge is upheld, client pays ordinary interest only (**no moratorium interest**); within 45 calendar days after the dictamen the full file must be made available; **no reporting of the disputed amount as overdue to credit bureaus** while unresolved; failure to answer → CONDUSEF fine equal to the claimed amount — [LTOSF (Profeco PDF)](https://www.profeco.gob.mx/juridico/pdf/LTOSF.pdf); [LTOSF reforms index (Cámara de Diputados)](https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/ref/ltosf.htm)
- Escalation: complaints against collection agencies go through **REDECO** (Registro de Despachos de Cobranza), an electronic public tool — [CONDUSEF REDECO](https://eduweb.condusef.gob.mx/redeco/redeco.aspx). SIPRES/registries are governed by CONDUSEF's registry disposiciones — [DOF: Disposición en materia de Registros ante la CONDUSEF](https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/notas/docFuente/5668453)

**Argentina (BCRA "Protección de los Usuarios de Servicios Financieros", texto ordenado al 06/05/26, last Com. "A" 8433)** [primary text verified] — [BCRA T.O. PUSF (PDF)](https://www.bcra.gob.ar/archivos/Pdfs/texord/t-pusf.pdf)
- **Sec. 3.1.6** (Com. "A" 6664): every consulta/reclamo must be **definitively resolved within max 10 business days** (except refund cases under 2.3.5, regulator-granted longer terms, or duly justified external causes). Channels must include phone, internet, writing, post, e-mail; **"Se deberá contemplar un procedimiento de atención personalizado para aquellos clientes que lo soliciten."** Resolution must be **notified in writing**; e-mail allowed if the customer accepted that channel when filing.
- **Sec. 3.1.3** (Com. "A" 6458): claims register must record claim number, date, channel, reason, ID, branches, status (pending / provisional / definitive answer + answer text); numbers must be correlative; when filed by phone or web the **claim number must be given on the spot**, otherwise within **3 business days**; data kept **10 years**. Complaints about non-performance must be registered even if answered immediately.
- **Sec. 4.2.1**: if the answer is unsatisfactory or **10 business days** pass with no answer, the user may escalate to the BCRA (Gerencia Principal de Protección al Usuario de Servicios Financieros) via the BCRA's online "reclamos no resueltos" channel — also [BCRA — Cómo hacer un reclamo](https://www2.bcra.gob.ar/bcrayvos/Como-hacer-un-reclamo-ante-el-Banco-Central.asp)
- BCRA press: the 10-business-day limit replaced a previous 20-day limit [secondary] — [BCRA noticia](https://www.bcra.gob.ar/noticias/Proteccion-usuario-financiero.asp)

**Brazil (SAC / Ouvidoria / BCB / consumidor.gov.br / Procon)**
- **Decreto 11.034, de 5 de abril de 2022** (SAC rules; in force 180 days after publication, i.e., early Oct 2022) [primary text, via Câmara publication; article summary extracted by tool] — [Decreto 11.034 (Câmara)](https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/decret/2022/decreto-11034-5-abril-2022-792480-publicacaooriginal-164911-pe.html); [Planalto](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2019-2022/2022/decreto/d11034.htm):
  - Art. 13: consumer demands must be answered within **7 calendar days (dias corridos)** from registration; §2: answer must be "**clara, objetiva e conclusiva**" and **address all points** raised.
  - Art. 12: every demand gets a **protocol number**; history must be provided on request (within **5 calendar days**); call recordings kept **≥90 days**; records kept **≥2 years**.
  - Art. 10: forbidden to make the consumer **repeat the demand** after it is registered.
  - Art. 14 II: **cancellation requests take effect immediately**, regardless of outstanding payments.
  - Art. 1: applies to suppliers of services regulated by the federal Executive (regulators may set different deadlines). Previous decree (6.523/2008) used 5 business days [secondary] — [Conjur](https://www.conjur.com.br/2022-mai-10/opiniao-decreto-sac-inova-relacao-atendimento/)
- **Ouvidoria — Resolução CMN 4.860/2020** (23 Oct 2020): institutions serving individuals/micro & small firms must have an ouvidoria; answer deadline **≤10 business days**, extendable **once**, exceptionally and with justification, for an equal period; extensions capped at **10% of the month's complaints**; complainant must be told why — [Res. CMN 4.860 (ANCORD PDF)](https://www.ancord.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Resolucao-CMN-n-4.860.pdf); [summary (Estratégia)](https://www.estrategiaconcursos.com.br/blog/resolucao-cmn-4860/). Payment institutions: Res. BCB 28/2020 — [Credsystem ouvidoria report citing Res. BCB 28/2020](https://www.credsystem.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Relatorio-Ouvidoria-2o-semestre-2023.pdf)
- **BCB complaint (RDR)**: institution has **10 business days** to answer a complaint lodged with the BCB, extendable in exceptional cases [secondary, law-firm blog] — [Rosenbaum Advogados (2026)](https://www.rosenbaum.adv.br/como-reclamar-ao-banco-central-do-brasil/)
- **consumidor.gov.br**: company has **up to 10 days** to respond; consumer then has **up to 20 days** to rate it resolved/unresolved — [consumidor.gov.br — Como funciona](https://www.consumidor.gov.br/pages/principal/como-funciona)

- Candidate deterministic rules (synthetic, for the prototype):
  - `CO.COMPLAINT.SLA = 15 business days` (entity); `CO.DCF.ENTITY_REPLY = 8 bd`; `CO.DCF.DECISION = 8 bd`; `CO.DCF.FORWARD = 3 bd`.
  - `MX.UNE.SLA = 30 business days`; `MX.ACLARACION.FILING_WINDOW = 90 calendar days`; `MX.ACLARACION.DICTAMEN = 45 days (domestic) / 180 calendar days (abroad)`; `MX.ACLARACION.BLOCK_CREDIT_BUREAU_REPORTING = true`.

  - `BR.SAC.SLA = 7 calendar days`; `BR.OUVIDORIA.SLA = 10 business days (+1 extension ≤10 bd)`; `BR.CONSUMIDOR_GOV.SLA = 10 days`.
- A universal "formal response" template can satisfy all four: claim/protocol number, date/time received, each point answered, decision + legal/contractual basis, evidence attached (MX/AR card disputes), next escalation step (DCF/SFC; CONDUSEF; BCRA; Ouvidoria/BCB/consumidor.gov.br/Procon), and channel of notification.
- The agent should never ask a Brazilian customer to restate a registered demand (Decreto 11.034 art. 10) — useful for multi-turn/hand-off design (carry context to the human).

## 2. Card disputes & unrecognized charges: reporting windows, consumer liability, investigation deadlines, provisional credit; Visa/Mastercard chargeback families

### Takeaway
Mexico is the most prescriptive: **debit-card** unrecognized charges must be credited back by the **2nd banking business day** (Banxico Circular 3/2012 art. 19 Bis 3, as amended by Circular 14/2018) unless the bank proves two-factor authentication; plus the LTOSF 90-day/45-day aclaración procedure. Argentina's **Ley 25.065** gives a **30-day** window to challenge a credit-card statement with a **7/15-day** issuer response (60 days abroad). Colombia's **payment-reversal** regime (Ley 1480 art. 51 / Decreto 587/2016) gives **5 business days** to claim and **15 business days** to reverse for e-commerce fraud. Brazil has no statutory card-dispute clock; banks bear objective liability for third-party fraud (STJ Súmula 479). Visa/Mastercard: four reason-code families; cardholder window generally **120 days**.

### Cited Findings

**Mexico**
- **Banxico Circular 14/2018** (DOF 3 Oct 2018) amended **Circular 3/2012** (Disposiciones aplicables a las operaciones de las instituciones de crédito), adding arts. 19 Bis–19 Bis 5 on debit cards; in force **26 Mar 2019**; the 2nd-business-day credit obligation in force **26 Sep 2019** (4th business day before that) [primary text verified] — [DOF Circular 14/2018](https://www.dof.gob.mx/nota_detalle.php?codigo=5539863&fecha=03%2F10%2F2018):
  - **Art. 19 Bis 1**: bank must accept notices of (i) **theft/loss** and (ii) **unrecognized charges**, at the customer's choice, in person at any branch / customer-service channels, or via agreed electronic channels; **may not require any additional procedure**; must give a **reference number + date and time** of the notice — immediately if in person, **within 24 hours** if via electronic channels.
  - **Art. 19 Bis 2**: within **2 banking business days** the bank must make available a report stating the customer's liability scope before/after the notice, date/time received, and **investigation status**; the customer is **not liable for charges after a theft/loss notice**.
  - **Art. 19 Bis 3**: bank must **credit the account no later than the 2nd banking business day** after the notice when (I) the charges occurred in the **48 hours before a theft/loss notice** and were not made with the required authentication (art. 19 Bis fr. I), **or** (II) an unrecognized-charge claim is filed **within 90 days** of the charge. Exception: if within that period the bank delivers a **dictamen proving the charges used ≥2 authentication factors** (unless there was an operational failure, e.g., a duplicate charge). No extra paperwork may be demanded to credit.
  - **Art. 19 Bis 4**: the bank may **reverse the credit only** if it proves the operation was executed per art. 19 Bis fr. I (authenticated).
- **Credit cards**: Banxico **Circular 34/2010** (Reglas de tarjetas de crédito) contains an analogous 90-day claim / 2nd-business-day credit / 48-hour rule, per press and the compiled text [secondary; exact regla number not verified] — [Banxico Circular 34/2010 texto compilado](https://www.banxico.org.mx/marco-normativo/normativa-emitida-por-el-banco-de-mexico/circular-34-2010/%7B0C55B906-6DB4-6B88-FED0-67987E9FB3CC%7D.pdf); [El Universal](https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/cartera/economia/bancos-obligados-devolver-cargos-no-reconocidos-en-48-horas-banxico/)
- Popular framing (Aug 2026): "report within 48 hours → provisional credit within 2 business days; 90 days max to file; 45 days to resolve domestic / 180 international" [secondary]. This article also claims an unanswered claim after 45 days is "automatically granted" — **contradicted** by LTOSF art. 23 text, which instead provides a CONDUSEF fine equal to the claimed amount — [El Imparcial, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.elimparcial.com/dinero/2026/08/05/condusef-establece-que-los-bancos-deben-devolver-el-dinero-de-un-cargo-no-reconocido-en-dos-dias-habiles-si-el-reclamo-se-hace-en-48-horas-aunque-existe-un-plazo-de-90-dias-para-solicitar-la-aclaracion/?outputType=amp-type); [LTOSF](https://www.profeco.gob.mx/juridico/pdf/LTOSF.pdf)
- LTOSF art. 23 aclaración procedure (90 calendar days to file; 45-day dictamen; 180 calendar days abroad; right to withhold disputed payment; no credit-bureau reporting) — see Section 1 — [LTOSF](https://www.profeco.gob.mx/juridico/pdf/LTOSF.pdf)

**Colombia**
- **Ley 1480 de 2011 art. 51 + Decreto 587 de 2016** (DUR 1074/2015 arts. 2.2.2.51.x) [primary text verified] — [Decreto 587 de 2016 (Alcaldía de Bogotá)](https://www.alcaldiabogota.gov.co/sisjur/normas/Norma1.jsp?i=65906):
  - Applies to purchases via e-commerce (internet, PSE, call center, televenta, virtual store) paid by credit/debit card or other electronic payment instrument; **payment participants must reverse** when (1) the consumer is a **victim of fraud**, (2) the operation was **not requested**, (3) product not received, (4) not as ordered, (5) defective (art. 2.2.2.51.2). Partial reversal allowed (2.2.2.51.3).
  - Consumer must complain to the supplier **within 5 business days** of learning of the fraudulent/unrequested operation (2.2.2.51.4) and notify the issuer (same 5-day window per secondary summaries).
  - Issuer and participants have **15 business days** to make the reversal effective (2.2.2.51.8); defences: non-existent operation, lack of funds, failure to state the ground.
  - Only applies when merchant **and** issuer are domiciled in Colombia (Parágrafo 1, 2.2.2.51.1); not for face-to-face channels.

**Argentina**
- **Ley 25.065 (Tarjetas de Crédito)** [primary text verified] — [Ley 25.065 (InfoLEG)](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/55000-59999/55556/norma.htm):
  - Art. 26: holder may challenge the statement **within 30 days of receipt**, by simple note detailing the error.
  - Art. 27: issuer must **acknowledge within 7 days** and, **within the following 15 days**, correct the error or explain with copies of vouchers; **60 days** for operations abroad.
  - Art. 28: during the challenge the issuer **cannot block/impede card use** (within limit) and may only demand the minimum payment on unchallenged items.
  - Art. 29: holder has **7 days** to accept/reject the explanation (silence = tacit acceptance); if rejected, issuer must resolve with reasons within **10 business days**, then judicial action is open.
  - Art. 30: paying the minimum does not imply accepting the statement.
- **BCRA PUSF sec. 2.3.5** [primary text verified]: any amount charged improperly (fees without disclosure, above agreed, promotions mis-applied, etc.) must be refunded within **10 business days** of the claim (or **5 business days** after the bank/SEFYC detects it), with reasonable expenses and compensatory interest at **1.5× the average 30–59-day term-deposit rate**, credited automatically to the customer's account and notified — [BCRA T.O. PUSF](https://www.bcra.gob.ar/archivos/Pdfs/texord/t-pusf.pdf)
- **"Pull" immediate transfers**: customer has **60 days** to dispute a debit; the debited entity must **reimburse within 3 business days** and automatically charges back the originating entity — reported as **Com. "A" 7996 (30 Apr 2024)** [secondary; Com. number/date not verified against BCRA text] — [Bomchil](https://bomchil.com/noticia-y-publicacio/transferencias-pull-nuevas-reglamentaciones-para-el-ingreso-de-fondos-a-cuentas-propias/); [El Litoral](https://www.ellitoral.com/economia/transferencias-instantaneas-pull-nuevas-reglas-requisitos-obtener-consentimiento_0_YiHVSEdopt.html)

**Brazil**
- No statutory card-dispute clock found. **STJ Súmula 479** (DJe 1 Aug 2012): "As instituições financeiras respondem objetivamente pelos danos gerados por fortuito interno relativo a fraudes e delitos praticados por terceiros no âmbito de operações bancárias" — [STJ Súmula 479](https://processo.stj.jus.br/jurisprudencia/externo/informativo/?acao=pesquisar&sumula=479); [STJ arquivo](https://arquivocidadao.stj.jus.br/index.php/sumula-479-2)
- Card complaints therefore run under the SAC (7 calendar days) / Ouvidoria (10 business days) clocks — see Section 1.

**Visa / Mastercard (network level, summary)**
- Visa Claims Resolution families: **10.x Fraud** (10.1–10.5; "I didn't make this purchase"), **11.x Authorization** (11.1–11.3), **12.x Processing Errors** (duplicate, wrong amount, etc.), **13.x Consumer Disputes** (13.1–13.9; goods/services) — [Chargeback.io — Visa categories](https://www.chargeback.io/blog/visa-chargeback-reason-codes); 10.4 card-absent fraud is the code eligible for **Compelling Evidence 3.0** (two prior undisputed transactions 120–365 days old matching IP/device) — same source.
- Visa cardholder dispute window: generally **120 days** after processing date (some issues shorter, e.g., 75 days; future-delivery up to 540 days from purchase) — [Chargebacks911 — Visa time limits](https://chargebacks911.com/chargeback-rules/chargeback-time-limits/visa-chargeback-time-limit/)
- Mastercard families: **Fraud** (e.g., 4837 No Cardholder Authorization), **Authorization** (e.g., 4808), **Point-of-Interaction Error**, **Cardholder Dispute** (4853 catch-all) — [Chargeback.io — Mastercard categories](https://www.chargeback.io/blog/mastercard-chargeback-reason-codes)

- Synthetic rules:

  - `CO.ECOM_FRAUD.REVERSAL_CLAIM_WINDOW = 5 business days`; `CO.ECOM_FRAUD.REVERSAL_SLA = 15 business days`; `CO.ECOM_FRAUD.SCOPE = domestic merchant & issuer, non-present channels`.
  - `BR.CARD_FRAUD.LIABILITY_DEFAULT = bank (objective liability, Súmula 479)`; route through SAC/Ouvidoria SLAs.
  - Network layer: classify disputes into Fraud / Authorization / Processing / Consumer-dispute; warn if >120 days since processing date.
- Mexico's "48-hour" popular rule is a simplification; a prototype should encode the actual two-branch condition from art. 19 Bis 3.

## 3. Brazil Pix: MED, MED 2.0 and the self-service "botão de contestação" (as of 2026)

### Takeaway
Pix fraud refunds run through the **MED**: the victim can contest **up to 80 days** after the Pix; funds at the receiving institution are **blocked immediately**, banks have **up to 7 days** to analyse, and refunds are due **within 96 hours** of a fraud finding (≈**≤11 days** end-to-end per BCB). Since **1 Oct 2025** banks must offer an in-app, **no-human-needed** contest button; **MED 2.0 (Res. BCB 493/2025)** — multi-layer tracing and recovery from accounts beyond the first recipient — became **mandatory on 2 Feb 2026**.

### Cited Findings

## 4. Card blocking and fraud notification (24/7 blocking, proactive alerts)

### Takeaway
Argentina explicitly mandates a **24-hour phone line** for theft/loss reports with time-stamped correlative numbers (Ley 25.065 art. 51) and **same-day notice** when a bank itself blocks a product for security (BCRA PUSF 2.3.6). Mexico requires banks to accept theft/loss notices through branches or agreed digital channels, issue a reference immediately/≤24h, and removes customer liability after the notice. Brazil's SAC must be reachable **24/7**. Colombia's Ley 2300 explicitly exempts **fraud alerts** from collection-contact limits. No verified rule makes proactive fraud alerts universally mandatory.

### Cited Findings
- **Argentina — Ley 25.065 art. 51**: issuer must run a **telephone reporting system operating 24 hours a day** for stolen/lost cards, registering each report with **time and correlative number communicated to the caller on the spot** [primary text verified] — [Ley 25.065](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/55000-59999/55556/norma.htm)
- **Argentina — BCRA PUSF 2.3.6** (Com. "A" 8203, 27 Feb 2025): when security measures **block** debit/credit cards or home banking, the bank must (2.3.6.1) **inform the user the same day** by e-mail/phone with reasons and rehabilitation channels, and (2.3.6.2) allow **rehabilitation** via an electronic channel **and** in person at any branch [primary text verified] — [BCRA T.O. PUSF](https://www.bcra.gob.ar/archivos/Pdfs/texord/t-pusf.pdf)
- **Argentina — Com. "A" 8473** (issued 3 Sep 2026; BO 10 Sep 2026): BCRA fraud-risk **score per CUIL/CUIT** for immediate transfers, mandatory input for onboarding, transaction monitoring and periodic reviews; administrators have 120 days; entities 60/90 days after technical docs; "**las decisiones no podrán fundarse exclusivamente en el score**" — [Abogados.com.ar](https://abogados.com.ar/nuevo-score-de-riesgo-de-fraude-del-bcra-para-transferencias-inmediatas-com-a-8473/40015); [SIAP blog](https://siap.blogdelcontador.com.ar/normativa/comunicacion-bcra-a-8473-2026/)
- **Mexico — Circular 3/2012 arts. 19 Bis 1–2** (via Circular 14/2018): theft/loss notice accepted in person or via agreed digital channels; no extra procedure; reference number + date/time immediately (branch) or ≤24h (digital); customer **not liable for charges after the notice** [primary text verified] — [DOF Circular 14/2018](https://www.dof.gob.mx/nota_detalle.php?codigo=5539863&fecha=03%2F10%2F2018)
- **Mexico — alerts**: CONDUSEF *recommends* users activate bank alert systems to detect and report unrecognized charges within the 48-hour window (recommendation, not a mandate found) — [CONDUSEF — ¿Cargos no reconocidos?](https://www.condusef.gob.mx/?idc=1731&idcat=3&p=contenido); [El Financiero](https://www.elfinanciero.com.mx/mis-finanzas/2022/07/27/cargos-no-reconocidos-en-tu-tarjeta-esto-es-lo-que-debes-hacer-segun-la-condusef/)
- **Brazil — Decreto 11.034 art. 4**: SAC access must be available **uninterruptedly 24 hours a day, 7 days a week** (digital); human phone service **≥8 h/day** (art. 5) — [Decreto 11.034 (Câmara)](https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/decret/2022/decreto-11034-5-abril-2022-792480-publicacaooriginal-164911-pe.html); [Projuris summary](https://www.projuris.com.br/blog/lei-do-sac/)
- **Colombia — Ley 2300 de 2023 art. 8**: contacts to confirm monetary operations, send requested information or "**generar alertas sobre transacciones fraudulentas, inusuales o sospechosas**" are **exempt** from the law's channel/hour/frequency limits [primary text verified] — [Ley 2300 de 2023 (Función Pública)](https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?i=213990)

### Inferences
- Synthetic rules: `ALL.CARD_BLOCK.SELF_SERVICE = available 24/7` (hard requirement in AR by law; strongly implied for BR by SAC 24/7; best practice for MX/CO); `AR.BANK_INITIATED_BLOCK.NOTIFY = same day + reasons + reactivation channels`; `MX.THEFT_NOTICE.LIABILITY_SHIFT = from notice timestamp` (agent must timestamp the block/notice and issue a reference number); `CO.FRAUD_ALERT.EXEMPT_FROM_CONTACT_LIMITS = true`.
- The agent's "block card" tool must return a correlative reference + ISO timestamp to the user in-session (satisfies AR art. 51 and MX 19 Bis 1 at once).

### Gaps
- Colombia: no verified SFC rule mandating 24/7 card-blocking channels or mandatory transaction SMS alerts (a search result attributing a "real-time notification via two channels" rule appears to be a Honduras CNBS circular, not Colombia — excluded).
- Brazil: no BCB rule found specifically mandating 24/7 card blocking or proactive fraud alerts for cards.
- Mexico: CNBV Circular Única de Bancos transaction-notification requirements not researched.

## 5. Debt-collection conduct rules (for a collections / payment-plan agent)

### Takeaway
Hard, encodable limits exist in **Colombia** (Ley 2300/2023: Mon–Fri 07:00–19:00, Sat 08:00–15:00, no Sundays/holidays, max once per day and one channel per week, no contacting references, no home/work visits) and **Mexico** (CONDUSEF 2014 disposiciones: 07:00–22:00 in the debtor's time zone, full identification at first contact, written payment agreements, long list of prohibitions, 30-business-day complaint SLA via REDECO). **Brazil** (CDC art. 42/42-A) and **Argentina** (Ley 24.240 art. 8 bis; BCRA trato digno) set conduct standards (no ridicule, threats, fake judicial appearance) but no national contact-hour limits were found.

### Cited Findings
- **Colombia — Ley 2300 de 2023 ("Dejen de fregar")**, dated **10 Jul 2023**, in force **3 months after promulgation** (art. 10) [primary text verified] — [Ley 2300 (Función Pública)](https://www.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?i=213990):
  - Art. 1: applies to SFC-supervised entities **and anyone collecting** directly, via third parties or by assignment.
  - Art. 2: contact only through **channels the consumer authorised**.
  - Art. 3: once direct contact is established, **no contact via multiple channels in the same week, nor more than once the same day**; hours **Mon–Fri 7:00–19:00, Sat 8:00–15:00**, **no contact Sundays/holidays**; other hours only if the consumer requests it in a separate instrument after signing the contract.
  - Art. 4: **never contact personal or other references**; co-debtors/guarantors under the same rules.
  - Art. 6: **no collection visits** to home or workplace (exceptions: microcredit/agro credit with express authorisation; unreachable consumers).
  - Art. 7: must **not ask the consumer why they defaulted** (may ask about payment alternatives).
  - Art. 8: exemptions for transaction confirmations, requested info, fraud alerts.
  - Art. 9: sanctions by SFC and SIC.
  - Note: the gestor normativo metadata lists entry-into-force 10 Jul 2023, conflicting with art. 10's three-month vacatio; the law text controls.
- **Mexico — "Disposiciones de carácter general aplicables a las entidades financieras en materia de Despachos de Cobranza"** (CONDUSEF, **DOF 07/10/2014**; 90 calendar days to comply) [primary text verified] — [DOF](https://www.dof.gob.mx/nota_detalle_popup.php?codigo=5362845); [CONDUSEF press](https://www.condusef.gob.mx/?p=contenido&idc=771&idcat=1):
  - Disposición **CUARTA** (financial entity responsible for its agencies): identify the debtor before first contact; at first contact state agency name + agent, address/phone, creditor, contract, **amount and calculation date**, conditions to settle, the entity's complaint unit contact, and that **payments go only to the creditor entity**; be respectful; **contact only 07:00–22:00 in the debtor's time zone**; **document payment/restructuring agreements in writing** (breakdown of principal, ordinary & default interest, discounts/quita, whether it settles the debt, account for payment).
  - Prohibitions (CUARTA VII): names resembling public institutions; **hidden/"private" caller ID**; threatening/offending/intimidating debtor, family, co-workers or others; **collecting from third parties** incl. references (except co-obligors/guarantors); documents that **look like court papers**; blacklists/public notices; **receiving payment directly**; negotiating with minors or elderly (unless they are the debtor); using contact data other than those provided.
  - QUINTA: in-house collection should follow the same rules; SEXTA: portfolio sale contracts must pass these obligations to the buyer.
  - TERCERA III: entity must answer collection complaints (direct or via CONDUSEF) within **30 business days**; DÉCIMA SEGUNDA: CONDUSEF forwards REDECO complaints within **3 business days**.
  - Fines reported at **MXN 13,458–134,580** [secondary] — [El Imparcial, 10 Apr 2026](https://www.elimparcial.com/dinero/2026/04/10/si-recibes-llamadas-de-cobranza-en-mexico-estas-son-las-nuevas-reglas-oficiales-que-limitan-horarios-prohiben-abusos-y-segun-la-condusef-pueden-ser-sancionados-con-hasta-134-mil-pesos/)
- **Brazil — CDC (Lei 8.078/1990)** [primary text verified] — [CDC compilado (Planalto)](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8078compilado.htm):
  - Art. 42: in collecting debts, the defaulting consumer "**não será exposto a ridículo, nem será submetido a qualquer tipo de constrangimento ou ameaça**"; sole paragraph: consumer charged an undue amount is entitled to **double repayment** of the excess plus correction and interest, save justifiable error.
  - Art. 42-A: all collection documents must show the supplier's **name, address and CPF/CNPJ**.
- **Argentina — Ley 24.240 art. 8 bis** (added by Ley 26.361, 2008) [primary text verified]: dignified treatment; no conduct placing consumers in "situaciones vergonzantes, vejatorias o intimidatorias"; "**En los reclamos extrajudiciales de deudas, deberán abstenerse de utilizar cualquier medio que le otorgue la apariencia de reclamo judicial**"; civil fine (art. 52 bis) extends jointly to whoever acts for the supplier — [Ley 24.240 texto actualizado (InfoLEG)](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/0-4999/638/texact.htm)
- **Argentina — BCRA PUSF 2.6 "Trato digno"**: no discriminatory policies/practices; must avoid the practices in 2.4 toward all persons, even non-customers [primary text verified] — [BCRA T.O. PUSF](https://www.bcra.gob.ar/archivos/Pdfs/texord/t-pusf.pdf)

### Inferences
- Synthetic rules for a collections agent:
  - `CO.COLLECT.WINDOW = Mon–Fri 07:00–19:00, Sat 08:00–15:00 America/Bogota; Sun/holidays = blocked`; `CO.COLLECT.MAX_PER_DAY = 1`; `CO.COLLECT.CHANNELS_PER_WEEK = 1 (after direct contact)`; `CO.COLLECT.CONTACT_REFERENCES = forbidden`; `CO.COLLECT.ASK_REASON_FOR_DEFAULT = forbidden`.
  - `MX.COLLECT.WINDOW = 07:00–22:00 in debtor's local time zone`; `MX.COLLECT.FIRST_CONTACT_DISCLOSURES = [agency, agent, creditor, contract, amount+date, settlement terms, complaint unit, pay-only-to-creditor]`; `MX.COLLECT.AGREEMENT_IN_WRITING = true`; `MX.COLLECT.THIRD_PARTIES = forbidden except co-obligors`.
  - `BR.COLLECT.NO_THREAT_OR_SHAMING = true`; `BR.COLLECT.DOC_IDENTIFIERS = [name, address, CNPJ]`.
  - `AR.COLLECT.NO_JUDICIAL_APPEARANCE = true`; `AR.COLLECT.DIGNIFIED_TREATMENT = true`.
- A safe cross-country default for a prototype: intersect the windows (e.g., weekdays 08:00–19:00 local, Sat 08:00–15:00, no Sun/holidays), one contact/day, never mention third parties, never threaten legal action unless a real filing exists.

### Gaps
- Brazil and Argentina: no national rule on collection contact hours/frequency found (some states/provinces and Procon practice may impose limits; not researched). Brazil's Lei 14.181/2021 (superendividamento) not researched.
- Mexico: whether a newer (2025–2026) CONDUSEF reform modified the 2014 collection disposiciones was not confirmed; the Apr 2026 article calls them "new rules" but cites the same content.

## 6. Data protection, automated decisions and AI/chatbot rules; requirements to offer a human agent

### Takeaway
Only **Brazil (LGPD art. 20)** grants a general right to **review solely automated decisions** (credit/consumption profiling included) — but since Lei 13.853/2019 the review need **not** be by a human. **Mexico's new LFPDPPP (in force 21 Mar 2025)** adds a right to **object** to automated processing with adverse/significant effects. **Colombia** relies on SIC guidance (Circular Externa 002/2024; concept 25-86338 of Apr 2025). **Argentina's** Ley 25.326 art. 20 covers only judicial/administrative decisions, though BCRA's 2026 fraud-score rule forbids decisions based *solely* on the score. No 2024–2026 financial-regulator rule specific to GenAI chatbots was found in any of the four countries. Human-agent access is explicit in **Brazil** (SAC human phone service ≥8h/day; first menu must offer complaint and cancellation) and **Argentina** (personalised attention on request).

### Cited Findings

**Brazil**
- **LGPD (Lei 13.709/2018) art. 20** (wording by Lei 13.853/2019): data subject may request **review of decisions taken solely on automated processing** that affect their interests, including decisions defining **personal, professional, consumer and credit profile**; §1 controller must provide, on request, **clear and adequate information on the criteria and procedures** used (subject to trade secrets); §2 if refused on trade-secret grounds, the ANPD may **audit** for discriminatory aspects [primary text verified] — [LGPD (Planalto)](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2018/lei/l13709.htm)
- The original text required review "por pessoa natural"; MP 869/2018 → Lei 13.853/2019 removed it; Congress **maintained the veto on 2 Oct 2019**, so review by a human is **not mandatory** — [Teletime](https://teletime.com.br/02/10/2019/congresso-mantem-veto-e-lgpd-fica-sem-revisao-humana-de-decisoes-automatizadas/); [Serpro](https://www.serpro.gov.br/lgpd/noticias/2019/congresso-aprecia-veto-ao-artigo-20-da-lgpd)
- **Decreto 11.034/2022 art. 5**: telephone SAC must offer service **by a human ≥8 hours/day**; the **first menu must include "reclamação" and "cancelamento"**; regulators may require longer human hours — [Decreto 11.034 (Câmara)](https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/decret/2022/decreto-11034-5-abril-2022-792480-publicacaooriginal-164911-pe.html); [Projuris](https://www.projuris.com.br/blog/lei-do-sac/)
- **BCB**: 2025–2026 regulatory agenda lists **study of risks and impacts of AI use by financial institutions**; the BCB does **not plan, for now, a specific AI regulation** — [Machado Meyer](https://www.machadomeyer.com.br/pt/inteligencia-juridica/publicacoes-ij/bancario-seguros-e-financeiro-ij/banco-central-conheca-a-agenda-regulatoria-para-2025-2026); [Convergência Digital](https://convergenciadigital.com.br/governo/bc-prioriza-pix-open-finance-inteligencia-artificial-e-tokenizacao/). Data-governance pressure from **Resolução Conjunta 18/2025** (statutory director accountable for quality of data sent to BCB) — [EY, Aug 2026](https://www.ey.com/pt_br/newsroom/2026/08/nova-resolucao-bc-adocao-ia-exigem-gestao-eficiente-dados-instituicoes-financeiras)

**Mexico**
- **New LFPDPPP** published **DOF 20 Mar 2025**, in force **21 Mar 2025**, repealing the 2010 law; **Secretaría Anticorrupción y Buen Gobierno** replaces INAI as authority; new ground to **object** when data undergo **automated processing producing adverse legal effects or significantly affecting rights/freedoms** (profiling without human involvement) — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/es/publications/mexicos-new-federal-data-protection-law-what-it-means-for-companies); [DOF original text (Cámara)](https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/ref/lfpdppp/LFPDPPP_orig_20mar25.pdf); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=f633c3f8-1591-4a91-a2f2-255b97a107e1)

- **BCRA Com. "A" 8473 (Sep 2026)**: fraud-risk decisions "**no podrán fundarse exclusivamente en el score**" — [Abogados.com.ar](https://abogados.com.ar/nuevo-score-de-riesgo-de-fraude-del-bcra-para-transferencias-inmediatas-com-a-8473/40015)

### Inferences
- Synthetic rules:
  - `ALL.AI.DISCLOSE_BOT = true` (not a verified legal mandate in these four countries; supported by SIC transparency principles and good practice — label as policy choice).

## 7. Language obligations (Portuguese for Brazil) and cross-border implications

### Takeaway
Brazilian consumer information must be in **Portuguese** (CDC art. 31) — so a Brazil-facing agent must serve pt-BR. Argentina requires sale documents in **castellano** (Ley 24.240 art. 10) and forbids discriminating against foreign consumers (art. 8 bis). Several consumer rules have **cross-border carve-outs or longer clocks** (MX 180 calendar days for foreign-operation disputes; AR 60 days; CO payment reversal only for domestic merchant+issuer; MX collection hours by debtor time zone).

- **Brazil — Decreto 11.034 art. 6**: accessibility of SAC channels for **persons with disabilities** is mandatory — [Decreto 11.034 (Câmara)](https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/decret/2022/decreto-11034-5-abril-2022-792480-publicacaooriginal-164911-pe.html)
- **Argentina — Ley 24.240 art. 10**: sale documents "La redacción debe ser hecha en **idioma castellano**, en forma completa, clara y fácilmente legible"; **art. 8 bis**: no differentiation against **foreign consumers** on price/quality/other relevant aspects without authority approval [primary text verified] — [Ley 24.240 (InfoLEG)](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/0-4999/638/texact.htm)
- **Mexico — LTOSF art. 23 II**: disputes on operations **made abroad** get up to **180 calendar days** for the dictamen (vs. 45 days domestic) [primary text verified] — [LTOSF](https://www.profeco.gob.mx/juridico/pdf/LTOSF.pdf)
- **Argentina — Ley 25.065 art. 27**: correction term extends to **60 days** for operations abroad [primary text verified] — [Ley 25.065](https://servicios.infoleg.gob.ar/infolegInternet/anexos/55000-59999/55556/norma.htm)
- **Colombia — Decreto 587/2016 (art. 2.2.2.51.1 par. 1)**: payment-reversal rules only apply when **merchant and card issuer are domiciled in Colombia** [primary text verified] — [Decreto 587 de 2016](https://www.alcaldiabogota.gov.co/sisjur/normas/Norma1.jsp?i=65906)
- **Mexico — collection disposiciones (CUARTA IV)**: contact hours 07:00–22:00 "**de acuerdo a los husos horarios en que se encuentre el Deudor**" [primary text verified] — [DOF 07/10/2014](https://www.dof.gob.mx/nota_detalle_popup.php?codigo=5362845)

### Inferences
- Synthetic rules: `BR.LANGUAGE = pt-BR mandatory for all customer-facing content, disclosures and responses`; `AR.LANGUAGE = es (castellano) for contractual/sale documents`; `MX/CO.LANGUAGE = es (default; statutory basis not verified here)`; `LANGUAGE = customer's jurisdiction, not the bank's HQ`.
- Time-zone handling must use the **customer's** local zone (explicit in MX; Brazil and Mexico have multiple zones), and SLA clocks must use the **jurisdiction's** business-day calendar.
- Cross-border card transactions should switch dispute SLAs (MX 180 calendar days; AR 60 days) and exclude Colombia's Decreto 587 reversal path for foreign merchants (fall back to network chargeback: 120 days).
